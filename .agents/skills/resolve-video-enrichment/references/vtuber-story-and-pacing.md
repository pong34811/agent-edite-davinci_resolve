# Thai VTuber story selection and comic timing

## Evidence boundary
- This is an editorial playbook, not a measured claim about all Thai viewers or a guarantee of YouTube growth.
- Its story priorities adapt the local `docs/guides/editorial-decision-guide.md`: emotion/story, clarity, rhythm, eye trace, continuity, then coverage variety.
- Preserving breathing room and choosing motivated split edits are supported by the Editor's Guide, lessons 1 and 3.[6]
- The VTuber-specific applications and examples below are testable editorial hypotheses, not platform requirements.
- No creator footage or channel analytics was reviewed to build this chapter; do not label the examples proven for a particular channel.

## Define the viewer promise
- Write one sentence: "คนดูจะได้เห็น [เหตุการณ์] และรู้สึก [อารมณ์] เพราะ [จุดพลิก/เสน่ห์ของคนพูด]".
- Choose the primary audience: existing fans, viewers of the game/topic, or newcomers to this VTuber.
- For newcomers, make the relationship, stakes, and referents legible without a lore lecture.
- For fans, retain signature speech, running jokes, and character habits when they carry the appeal.
- Do not assume every Thai audience wants fast shouting, profanity, or constant memes.
- Preserve the creator's public persona; a calm, affectionate moment can be stronger than the loudest scream.

## Candidate selection rubric
| Dimension | Strong candidate | Weak candidate / repair |
|---|---|---|
| Self-contained premise | Viewer can identify the immediate situation | Needs a long previous stream: choose another range or add minimal approved context |
| Change | Expectation, plan, emotion, or relationship changes | Same intensity throughout: find a genuine turn, do not fabricate one |
| Payoff | Clear action, line, reveal, or expressive reaction | Ending is missing: extend the source review or reject |
| Personality | Creator-specific behavior makes the beat distinctive | Interchangeable scream: prefer a more revealing moment |
| Legibility | Picture and audio explain the event together | Buried speech/HUD: test a layout or flag unusable |
| Integrity | Honest context and rights are clear | Restricted source, false implication, private information: hold |

- Use the rubric to compare candidates, not as an automatic winner score.
- Review beyond each proposed boundary so teasing, roleplay, sarcasm, and corrections remain intelligible.
- Scan the whole source rather than selecting every highlight from its opening minutes.
- Record evidence and uncertainty per candidate; subtitles alone cannot prove visual surprise or timing.
- A complete source inventory and exact ranges must precede any claim that all material was reviewed.

## Build the beat map
1. Hook: show or say the immediate promise, contradiction, or risk.
2. Minimum context: include only what the next beat requires.
3. Escalation: reveal why the promise is harder, stranger, sweeter, or funnier than expected.
4. Payoff: let the decisive line, action, or reveal finish.
5. Reaction/button: retain the beat that makes the creator's personality land, then end cleanly.
- A short need not use all five as separate shots; these are functions, not compulsory time blocks.
- If a cold open borrows a later reaction, return to honest context; do not falsely imply a different cause or speaker.
- Put compelling material early, but do not reveal every punchline in the opening caption.
- Do not stretch a complete moment to fill a preset duration or force every clip into Shorts.

## Choose rhythm by the moment
| Moment | Preserve | Compress | Helpful finish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confident prediction goes wrong | Promise, visible contradiction, honest reaction | Setup that repeats what viewers already know | One reveal accent or short punch-in |
| Unexpected game event | Cause and enough HUD/world detail to identify it | Routine traversal/loading | Alternate game evidence and avatar response |
| Chat teasing / banter | Relevant chat prompt, who responds, tone | Unrelated chat scrolling | Clear speaker/context cue, not fake dialogue |
| Shy / affectionate reaction | Hesitation, expression, soft laugh | Unrelated lead-in | Restraint; silence or subtle music rather than a crash sting |
| Horror surprise | Suspense and release | Empty repetition before suspense | Preserve original impact, avoid louder stacked jump-scare audio |
| Skillful play | Readable action and result | Menus or unrelated rounds | Prioritize the play over avatar zoom |
| Callback / lore joke | The one premise that unlocks it | Explanations only veterans need | Brief approved context, then let the real line land |

## Timing decisions
- Cut on a change in thought, action, or emotion, not on a rigid metronome.
- Preserve the short silence that sets up an absurd answer; remove silence only after deciding it carries no meaning.
- Do not cut a breath or trailing laugh so tightly that speech becomes mechanical.
- Let the viewer notice a reaction before replacing it with another visual demand.
- Use sound to lead an incoming shot (J-cut) when it clarifies the new idea; let outgoing sound continue under reaction picture (L-cut) when truthful.[6]
- Rewatch at normal speed; timeline zoom and frame stepping can make an unreadably short hold look adequate.
- Compare a restrained version with a more energetic version only when the user approves variants; do not overwrite the chosen cut.

## Every effect needs a job
- Before adding an effect, name its job: focus attention, clarify a referent, emphasize a turn, or release tension.
- Use the creator's real face/avatar/game reaction before reaching for an external meme.
- Prefer one dominant emphasis at a payoff; zoom, caption change, flash, shake, GIF, and sting together often compete.
- Avoid repeated zooms that create nausea or make the avatar look permanently cramped.
- Pick cue times from verified speech and performance, not evenly spaced positions on the timeline.
- An asset can be familiar without being repetitive; rotate only within the right emotional meaning.
- If removing an effect improves clarity without losing the joke, remove it in the proposed variant.
- The existing exemplar determines style density; an old cue-per-minute value is not a universal target.

## Illustrative Thai beat designs
These are fictional prompts for practice, not quotes from a real VTuber.

### Confident fail
- Hook/premise: the speaker confidently announces an easy move.
- Context: show the target or obstacle, not an unrelated reaction close-up.
- Turn: preserve the actual failed action and its immediate sound.
- Payoff: keep the speaker's realization; a single short caption can mark it after it is spoken.
- Button: end after the self-aware reaction, before the next unrelated conversation.
- Test: compare original game sound alone against one light accent; choose what makes the causal beat clearer.

### Quiet chemistry
- Hook/premise: a simple question receives an unexpectedly affectionate answer.
- Context: identify the speakers only as much as a newcomer needs.
- Turn: retain the hesitation and visible change in expression.
- Payoff: let the response breathe; avoid a meme that changes warmth into ridicule.
- Button: a genuine laugh or small reply can close the moment without an outro card.
- Test: ask a viewer whether the intended emotion survived; completion alone cannot answer that.

## Match format to story
- Shorts: one immediately intelligible emotional arc; frame the first visible moment and payoff for a phone.
- Horizontal highlight: retain the game/chat geography when it explains the event better than a crop.
- Multi-moment highlight: sequence related mini-arcs with a reason for progression, not a dump of unrelated peaks.
- Crop/reframe only in an explicitly approved variant; protect the original aspect ratio.
- Keep an honest ending; a seamless loop is optional and must not interrupt the payoff or fake a new event.
- Place a call to action after value is delivered and only when requested; do not pad every short with a long outro.

## Review questions
- Can a newcomer explain what happened without reading a paragraph outside the clip?
- Does an existing fan still recognize the creator's rhythm and personality?
- Is the title/thumbnail promise actually fulfilled by the selected moment?
- Do picture, captions, and audio direct attention to the same important beat?
- Is any reaction displaced in a way that changes its target or meaning?
- What single change will the next comparable clip test, and which metric plus viewer feedback would support it?

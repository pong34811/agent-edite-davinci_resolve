# Post-edit YouTube research and comparative review

This is a manual workflow contract, not a claim of automated agent behavior. Research and review are proposals only; neither authorizes editing, uploading, changing a title or thumbnail, or publishing. There is no guarantee of reach or views and no access to YouTube's internal ranking formula.

## Verification limits

The repository's keyword-based documentation checks do not execute a research workflow or classifier. The planned fixture scenarios—fewer than three comparators, stale sources, missing transcript or analytics, absent permission, and Shorts versus long-form—remain manual and unverified as behavioral tests. A real pilot and agent entrypoint checks are still required; no automated handling is claimed.

## 1. At the start of each editing assignment

- Use the local date on the day the work starts as the research **as-of** date. Search and inspect current official YouTube guidance, recording each **source URL**, page title, access date, and relevant claim. Re-fetch rather than relying on prior notes; do not assert the algorithm changes daily.
- Official examples checked 2026-10-27: [How YouTube recommendations work](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387?hl=en) describes personalized surfaces and signals; [Understand your content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650?hl=en) describes appeal, engagement and satisfaction, with signals varying by format. These dates are historical evidence, not evidence of current guidance in a later job.
- If the web is unavailable or sources cannot be inspected, report **research unavailable**, the date of any older source, and the unverified claims; do not label stale guidance current. Carry forward only explicitly dated context.
- Establish authorized project/timeline/revision and channel/game identity with evidence; use confirmed, candidate, unknown or conflict rather than guessing. No credentials or private analytics access are implied.

## 2. After edit QC: compare public material

- Reuse the start-of-task official findings, noting their as-of date and any research unavailable status. Search public clips relevant to the confirmed game/topic, channel or similar audience, language and format. Select at least three comparators if available; prefer the same game/topic and format (Shorts or long-form), and similar audience/language. Record the search query, search date, candidate count, selection criteria, source URL, title, publication date and what was actually accessible and inspected.
- If fewer than 3 suitable comparators are found, state the actual number and reasons; do not invent candidates or stretch relevance. Record comparator timecode only for segments actually watched or heard and describe uninspected intervals. Metadata and a missing transcript do not establish what a video contains; label transcript unavailable and any unsupported content unknown.
- Compare observable hooks, clarity, pacing, context and payoff against actual target evidence, not popularity alone. High views do not prove an editing choice caused them; mark each proposed explanation a hypothesis and note confounders and possible downsides.
- Inspect the target clip only within the granted permission and scope. Do not send footage, audio, transcript or personal data to an external provider without explicit permission and scope. A public comparator does not grant rights to reuse it. No analytics account access or upload is implicit.

## 3. Report template (separate from any edit)

1. Target project/timeline ID and revision; channel/game identity, status and evidence, including conflicting or unknown evidence. Mark unavailable target footage or uninspected sections.
2. Official research: as-of, source URL, access date, claims and limitations. Include search query/date and comparator records with title, publication date, URL, inspected ranges/timecode and limitations; identify missing transcript or access.
3. Target evidence with timecode and clock basis, observed differences from each inspected comparator, and 1–3 prioritized proposals: each hypothesis, rationale, possible downside and one testable variable. Do not present a hypothesis as an approved preference.
4. Metrics: for Shorts consider stayed to watch and engaged views; for long-form consider counted thumbnail impressions/CTR and retention. Record the actual metric definition, comparable audience, traffic source, sample and window. If permission or analytics data is absent, explicitly say analytics unavailable/unknown; never fabricate figures or imply causality. Consult the packaging chapter for definitions and cohort safeguards.
5. State the target was **not edited** as a result of this review and **not published** by this workflow; list any separately authorized actions accurately. Record what remains unverified. A real pilot requires a user-selected clip, authorized access and actual inspected sources; this document alone proves none of these.

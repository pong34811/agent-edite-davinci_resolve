## 4. AI Video Editing

**Core principle:** AI accelerates editing decisions but human judgment selects the story.

**AI-assisted editing workflow:**
1. **Transcription:** Use AI transcription to identify dialogue beats and timestamps
2. **Highlight detection:** AI can flag potential moments (laughter, reactions, game events)
3. **Silence detection:** AI identifies dead air for tightening
4. **Scene detection:** AI segments long recordings into manageable chunks
5. **Human review:** Editor selects, arranges, and refines AI suggestions

**For Resolve + Hermes:**
- `resolve-media-analysis` provides transcription and scene detection
- `resolve-tighten-recording` automates dead-air removal
- `resolve-rough-cut` automates initial assembly
- Agent verifies AI suggestions against actual video content

**Pitfalls:**
- AI transcription of Thai speech over game audio is unreliable — verify manually
- AI highlight detection misses context — a laugh at a loss differs from a laugh at a win
- AI pacing suggestions are generic — adapt to each VTuber's rhythm

## 5. Video Pacing & Storytelling

**Core principle:** Pacing creates emotional rhythm — fast for excitement, slow for emotion.

**Pacing principles:**
- **Match energy to content:** Fast cuts for action/hype, longer takes for emotion/story
- **Vary rhythm:** Don't maintain one pace throughout — contrast creates interest
- **Cut on motion:** Transitions feel natural when cutting during movement
- **Hold for impact:** Let emotional reactions breathe — don't cut away too fast
- **Remove dead air:** Silence and filler slow pacing without adding value

**Storytelling structure:**
- **Setup → Conflict → Resolution** for story segments
- **Anticipation → Payoff → Reaction** for gaming moments
- **Question → Exploration → Answer** for zatsudan/talk content

**VTuber-specific:**
- Preserve the VTuber's reaction time — cutting away loses personality
- Chat reactions are part of the story — include them when relevant
- Game audio pacing affects edit pacing — work with the game's rhythm

## 6. Hook & Audience Retention

**Core principle:** The first 3 seconds determine whether viewers stay or leave.

**Hook types:**
| Type | Example | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Reaction hook | VTuber screaming/laughing | Emotional content |
| Question hook | "Can I beat this boss?" | Gaming content |
| Conflict hook | "She said what?!" | Drama/chat content |
| Achievement hook | "Finally, after 100 tries!" | Gaming progress |
| Mystery hook | "Something's wrong..." | Story/suspense |

**Retention techniques:**
- **Pattern interrupt:** Unexpected moment breaks viewer autopilot
- **Open loops:** Set up a question early, answer it later
- **Visual variety:** Change framing, add overlays, vary shot size
- **Caption timing:** Sync captions to speech rhythm for readability
- **Pacing承诺:** Deliver on the hook's promise within 15-30 seconds

**For Shorts specifically:**
- Hook must be immediate — no intro, no branding
- Every second must earn its place
- End with a complete moment, not a cliffhanger

## 12. Zoom, Cut & Transition

**Core principle:** Camera movement and transitions serve the story, not decoration.

**Zoom techniques:**
- **Push in:** Intensify emotion or focus attention
- **Pull out:** Reveal context or create distance
- **Zoom on reaction:** Highlight facial expressions and emotions
- **VTuber zoom focus:** Use Adjustment Clip to frame avatar (head-to-chest, 5-8% headroom)

**Cut techniques:**
- **Hard cut:** Default for most edits — clean and direct
- **J-cut:** Audio leads video — smooth transition into new content
- **L-cut:** Video leads audio — smooth transition out
- **Match cut:** Visual or audio similarity between shots
- **Jump cut:** Intentional jarring effect — use sparingly

**Transition rules:**
- Default to hard cuts — transitions are decorative, not narrative
- Use transitions only when they serve the story (time passage, location change)
- Match transition speed to content energy
- Avoid default transitions (crossfade, wipe) unless specifically requested

**For VTuber content:**
- Zoom focus on Adjustment Clip tracks, never modify source footage
- Cut between game and VTuber cam for reaction shots
- Use zoom to emphasize key moments without disrupting flow

## 13. Character Expression & Reactions

**Core principle:** The VTuber's face is the content — reactions are the payoff.

**Reaction types:**
| Reaction | Editing approach |
|---|---|
| Laughter | Hold, don't cut away — let it breathe |
| Shock/surprise | Zoom in, emphasize the moment |
| Frustration | Show the buildup, not just the outburst |
| Emotion/tears | Respect the moment — minimal editing |
| Chat excitement | Show chat + reaction together |

**Expression enhancement:**
- Zoom focus on Adjustment Clips to frame avatar expressions
- Cut to reaction shots at the peak moment
- Use SFX sparingly to enhance, not replace, genuine reactions
- Preserve the full arc: anticipation → trigger → reaction → recovery

**Pitfalls:**
- Don't cut away from genuine emotion for pacing
- Don't over-use reaction zooms — save them for peak moments
- Don't manufacture reactions that didn't happen
